Start with the work you do

The services you provide decide your obligations under the AML/CTF Act and Rules. Start with the work below, then check the exact definitions, the link to Australia and any exemptions.

Set up and support structures

You set up or restructure companies, trusts or SMSFs. You provide officeholders, a registered address or shelf companies.

Help clients buy, sell or finance

Assisting with property or a controlling interest in a legal entity or arrangement, or with equity or debt finance.

Handle client money or property

Trust account and other client-asset services that help a transaction proceed.

Not designated on their own

Tax returns, BAS, financial statements and bookkeeping are not designated services on their own. The detail of each engagement decides.

Which services and exceptions should we check?
  • assisting a client to plan or carry out a transaction to buy, sell or transfer real estate, or a controlling interest in a company, trust or other legal arrangement, other than under a court or tribunal order
  • receiving, holding and controlling, or managing a client's money or other property as part of a transaction, including through a trust account. Money held only as payment for your own services is excluded. The exemption for incidental holdings for non-designated work is conditional, including that the entity provides no other designated services beyond this client-property service
  • assisting with equity or debt financing for a company or legal arrangement
  • assisting to create or restructure a company, trust or other legal arrangement, including SMSF trusts and corporate trustees. Testamentary trusts are excluded
  • acting as, or arranging for someone to act as, a director, secretary, trustee, partner or nominee shareholder for a client
  • providing a registered office or principal place of business address, or selling a shelf company.

These services must be provided in the course of business and have a geographical link to Australia. A court-order exclusion does not automatically cover work done to obtain the order. Check the precise service and any applicable exemption.

When does the work come into scope?

AUSTRAC's test is whether your work directly advances the transaction or the creation of the structure. General advice that only influences what a client does is not enough. Advice detailed enough to enable the client to create a structure without further professional assistance may itself be in scope. The service starts when you act on instructions and a transaction or structure is in train.

Examples:

An accountant drafts and arranges execution of a trust deed to set up an SMSF: a designated service when the accountant accepts and acts on the instruction to create the trust.

An accountant gives tax advice on the implications of selling a company, with no buyer in view: not a designated service. Once the accountant acts on instructions to directly advance the sale to an identified buyer, it may be.

Client money sits in your trust account only to pay your own fees: not a designated service. Letting a client use the trust account like a bank account would be.

Recognise the risk in everyday work

Warning signs to look into, not findings of wrongdoing. Your team checks the facts and follows your procedures.

Warning sign

A structure with no clear purpose

A client wants several layers of entities but cannot say why.

Your team’s response
Ask the purpose, find out who owns and controls the structure, and rate the risk.

Warning sign

Ownership that stays unclear

A proposed trustee or officeholder will not say who is behind the arrangement.

Your team’s response
Get the missing ownership evidence. Follow your escalation steps before you go on.

Warning sign

Funds that do not fit the activity

An unrelated third party pays for the work, or the client asks for a refund to a different account.

Your team’s response
Find out who is paying and where the money comes from. Record the answer and rate the risk again.
