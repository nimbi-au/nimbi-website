Start with your services

Understand where the obligations apply.

Your designated services determine whether obligations under the AML/CTF Act and Rules apply. Check the work you perform, its connection to Australia and any relevant exclusions or exemptions.

Property and business matters

Assisting property transfers or transactions involving a controlling interest in a legal entity or arrangement.

Structures and finance

Entity creation, restructuring and finance, plus specified appointments and address services.

Client assets

Handling money or property to help a transaction proceed, including through trust accounts.

How do exclusions and privilege affect the work?

Advice, litigation or dispute resolution alone does not automatically create a designated service. Court-order exclusions and incidental client-money exceptions require a specific assessment. Legal professional privilege is distinct from confidentiality and affects reporting in defined circumstances; it does not remove enrolment or CDD duties. A licensed conveyancer's own communications are not privileged merely because they involve conveyancing.

Recognise the risk in everyday work.

Illustrative warning signs to investigate, not evidence of wrongdoing. Your team checks the facts and follows its procedures.

  • Unclear authority

    Instructions come from someone whose connection to the client is unclear.

    Establish the client, authority to act and relevant ownership.

  • Pressure to skip a step

    Urgency increases while identity or ownership information remains incomplete.

    Resolve gaps and follow the practice's escalation procedures.

  • Unexplained trust account transfers

    During a property matter, a client requests transfers through your trust account with no clear connection to the agreed legal work.

    Clarify the purpose and recipients, then follow your escalation procedures.

  • Rapid resale, unexplained price jump

    A property resold at a sharply higher price within days has no clear explanation for the increase in value.

    Ask about the rationale and supporting evidence; reassess the client's risk.
