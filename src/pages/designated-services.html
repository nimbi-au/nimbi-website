Are you providing a designated service?

It comes down to the services you provide, not the profession you're in.

You're a reporting entity only if you provide a designated service. Scroll through your sector below - if any line describes your work, the obligations attach to you, and they attached on 1 July 2026.

Real estate professionals

  • Brokering the sale, purchase or transfer of real estate for any party to the deal - buyer, seller, transferor or transferee - even where no money changes hands
  • Acting for a vendor as listing agent, or for a purchaser as buyer's agent
  • Selling or transferring real estate as a business without an independent agent brokering the deal - developers selling through in-house sales teams or off the plan
  • Brokering business sales where a company or trust changes hands - management rights, motels, franchises
Property management and leasing generally sit outside the net unless a sale or transfer is involved.

Accountants

  • Assisting a client to buy, sell or transfer real estate, or a company, business or trust
  • Receiving, holding or managing client money, accounts or property as part of a transaction - including through a trust account
  • Creating, restructuring or winding up companies, trusts and partnerships
  • Acting as, or arranging for someone to act as, a director, secretary, partner, trustee or nominee shareholder for a client entity
  • Providing a registered office or principal place of business address
  • Selling or transferring shelf companies
  • Helping to organise or carry out equity or debt financing for a company or trust
Preparing tax returns, financial statements, BAS or audits is not, by itself, a designated service. What brings a firm into the regime is moving client money, acting in purchases and sales, and setting up or changing structures.

Lawyers

  • Assisting clients to plan or carry out the sale, purchase or transfer of real estate
  • Assisting clients to buy, sell or transfer a business, company or trust
  • Receiving, holding or managing client money, securities or other property in the course of a transaction
  • Creating, restructuring or winding up companies, trusts and other legal arrangements
  • Acting as, or arranging for someone to act as, a director, secretary, trustee or partner of a client entity
  • Helping to organise equity or debt financing for a company, trust or other legal arrangement
  • Nominee shareholder, registered office and shelf company arrangements
Litigation and advocacy work generally falls outside the regime. The transactional side of practice is where obligations live.

Conveyancers

  • Assisting clients to plan or carry out the sale, purchase or transfer of real property
  • Assisting in business sales where a company or trust changes hands
  • Receiving, holding or paying out client money or property - including through a trust account - in the course of a transaction

  • Acting as, or arranging for someone to act as, a director, secretary, partner, trustee, nominee shareholder or power of attorney for a client entity
  • Selling or transferring shelf companies
  • Receiving, holding or managing client money, accounts or property in the course of a transaction
  • Providing registered office or business address services

Dealers in precious metals and stones

  • Buying or selling bullion as part of a bullion-dealing business - captured whatever the amount and however the customer pays
  • Buying or selling precious metals, stones, or jewellery, watches and similar items containing them, where $10,000 or more is paid in physical currency, virtual assets or a mix of both - in one transaction or several that appear linked
Bullion sales under $5,000 retail value are exempt from initial customer due diligence s 39E - but the sale is still a designated service, and full checks apply the moment enhanced due diligence is triggered.
