Start with the work you do

The services you provide decide your obligations under the AML/CTF Act and Rules. Start with the work below, then check the exact definitions, the link to Australia and any exemptions.

Sales agents

You broker a sale, purchase or transfer. Both the buyer and the seller are your customers, even if you act for one.

Buyers’ agents

You act for a buyer to find or buy property. The relationship usually starts when the agreement is signed.

Developers selling directly

You sell your own developments as a business, including off the plan, with no independent agent brokering the sale.

Outside these services

Rent-only property management is outside these designated services. Developing or building alone is not enough; how the property is sold matters.

Who is the customer, and when do checks start?
  • brokering the sale, purchase or transfer of real estate on behalf of a buyer, seller, transferee or transferor in the course of business. AUSTRAC treats a broker as someone who acts as an intermediary or agent for consideration, typically negotiating or finding a counterparty for a commission. This covers seller's agents and buyer's agents, and both the buyer and the seller are your customers for the same transaction
  • selling or transferring real estate in the course of a business of selling real estate, where no independent real estate agent brokers the sale: for example a developer selling house and land packages, off-the-plan apartments or subdivided lots, including through its own in-house agents or sales staff. The buyer is the customer. Where an independent agent brokers a developer's sale, that agent provides the brokerage service and this item does not apply to the developer for that sale. Developing or building on its own, and a one-off sale of your own premises, are not a business of selling real estate.

Real estate means freehold, leasehold interests of more than 30 years and land use entitlements; leases of 30 years or less, easements, mortgagee interests and dwellings not attached to land where the occupier owns the dwelling but leases the site are excluded; a leasehold interest over 30 years may itself qualify. Transfers for no consideration still count, and the Act applies where the service has a geographical link to Australia. A seller's agent starts providing the service to the seller when the agency agreement is signed, and to the buyer when the sale is reasonably expected to proceed, typically on exchange. A buyer's agent starts with the buyer when the agreement to find a property is signed. A developer selling direct starts when there is a commitment to sell, typically on entering the sale agreement. AUSTRAC accepts delayed initial due diligence in limited circumstances, such as after an auction where completing it first would disrupt the ordinary course of business and the extra risk is low; the delay provisions are conditional, so a developer selling off the plan should not assume it can wait until settlement.

Examples:

An agency lists and sells a house on a commission: a designated service, with the seller a customer from listing and the buyer a customer from exchange.

A buyers' agent signs an agreement to find a property for a client: a designated service from that signing, before any property is identified.

The same agency manages a rent roll: collecting rent, handling tenancies and maintenance are not designated services.

A developer sells apartments off the plan from its display suite through its own sales staff: a designated service, with each buyer a customer. If the same developer appoints an independent agent to broker the sales, the agent provides the brokerage service and the developer is not providing item 2 for those sales.

An owner sells their own business premises directly to a buyer, as a one-off: not a business of selling real estate, so not a designated service. A builder that constructs homes for a landowner and sells nothing is not providing a real estate designated service.

Recognise the risk in everyday work

Warning signs to look into, not findings of wrongdoing. Your team checks the facts and follows your procedures.

Warning sign

The person behind the buyer is unclear

A company buyer will not say who owns it or who is directing the purchase.

Your team’s response
Identify the owners and controllers, verify the authority to act, and record the risk rating.

Warning sign

Funding does not match the customer story

The money, or a third party paying, does not fit what you know about the customer.

Your team’s response
Ask for an explanation and evidence. Rate the risk again and escalate under your procedures.

Warning sign

The arrangement changes without explanation

The buyer or the source of funds changes late, with pressure to accept quickly.

Your team’s response
Review the new information and repeat any affected checks before you decide.
