AML/CTF for real estate professionals

Real estate agents, buyers' agents and developers selling their own developments, including off the plan, provide designated services. For developers, the scope check considers whether an independent agent brokers the sale. Rent-only property management is not a designated service.

When the rules may apply

The real estate services in table 5 of the Act are:

  • brokering the sale, purchase or transfer of real estate on behalf of a buyer, seller, transferee or transferor in the course of business. AUSTRAC treats a broker as someone who acts as an intermediary or agent for consideration, typically negotiating or finding a counterparty for a commission. This covers seller's agents and buyer's agents, and both the buyer and the seller are your customers for the same transaction
  • selling or transferring real estate in the course of a business of selling real estate, where no independent real estate agent brokers the sale: for example a developer selling house and land packages, off-the-plan apartments or subdivided lots, including through its own in-house agents or sales staff. The buyer is the customer. Where an independent agent brokers a developer's sale, that agent provides the brokerage service and this item does not apply to the developer for that sale. Developing or building on its own, and a one-off sale of your own premises, are not a business of selling real estate.

Real estate means freehold, leasehold interests of more than 30 years and land use entitlements; leases of 30 years or less, easements, mortgagee interests and dwellings on leased sites are excluded. Transfers for no consideration still count, and the Act applies where the service has a geographical link to Australia. A seller's agent starts providing the service to the seller when the agency agreement is signed, and to the buyer when the sale is reasonably expected to proceed, typically on exchange. A buyer's agent starts with the buyer when the agreement to find a property is signed. A developer selling direct starts when there is a commitment to sell, typically on entering the sale agreement. AUSTRAC accepts delayed initial due diligence in limited circumstances, such as after an auction where completing it first would disrupt the ordinary course of business and the extra risk is low; the delay provisions are conditional, so a developer selling off the plan should not assume it can wait until settlement.

Examples

An agency lists and sells a house on a commission: a designated service, with the seller a customer from listing and the buyer a customer from exchange.

A buyers' agent signs an agreement to find a property for a client: a designated service from that signing, before any property is identified.

The same agency manages a rent roll: collecting rent, handling tenancies and maintenance are not designated services.

A developer sells apartments off the plan from its display suite through its own sales staff: a designated service, with each buyer a customer. If the same developer appoints an independent agent to broker the sales, the agent provides the brokerage service and the developer is not providing item 2 for those sales.

An owner sells their own business premises directly to a buyer, as a one-off: not a business of selling real estate, so not a designated service. A builder that constructs homes for a landowner and sells nothing is not providing a real estate designated service.
