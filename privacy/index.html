Privacy Policy and Collection Notice

This policy explains how Nimbi Group Pty Ltd (ABN 36 701 758 018), trading as Nimbi ("Nimbi", "we", "us"), collects, uses, discloses and protects personal information. We carry out customer due diligence as an agent for businesses regulated under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006 (the AML/CTF Act), and the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles apply to that work whatever the size of our business. We apply the same standards to everything else we do, and this policy covers all of it.

Because of what we do, we handle personal information in two quite different situations, and this policy is split the same way. Part A covers people whose identity we verify, or whose name we screen, on behalf of a client. Part B covers our clients, prospective clients and website visitors.

The short version

If a business you're dealing with has asked us to verify your identity or screen your name, we're doing that because the law requires them to check who they're dealing with. We collect what the check needs, share the outcome with that business, keep the record for as long as the law requires, and don't use your information for anything beyond that check and our own record-keeping.

If you're a client or visiting our website, we collect what you give us, use it to respond and to deliver our services, never sell it, and you can ask us at any time what we hold and to correct it.

Part A - People we verify or screen on behalf of a client

Why we hold your information

A business you are dealing with - for example an accounting practice, real estate agency, law firm or conveyancer - may engage Nimbi to carry out customer due diligence on its behalf. The AML/CTF Act and its Rules require that business to identify and verify its customers before providing certain services, to work out who owns and controls any company, trust or other organisation it acts for, to identify anyone acting on a customer's behalf, and to keep monitoring the relationship for as long as it continues. So you may hear from us because you're the customer, or because you're connected to one - a director, a trustee, someone authorised to act for a company or trust, or someone who owns or controls it. Either way, we act for that business, and we hold your information for it and for that legal purpose.

The business should point you to this notice before or when your details are collected. If the information can't be collected, or your identity can't be verified, the business generally can't go ahead with the service you've asked for. If you have questions about why a check is needed, the business that asked for it is the right first contact: the obligation is theirs, and the decision to provide or decline their service is theirs too.

Sometimes a client asks us to screen the customers it already has rather than someone new. Your details then come to us from the client, not from you, and you may never hear from us directly. The client's own privacy notice should tell you that checks like this happen; this policy explains what we do with your information once it reaches us.

Where a client runs our verification journey under its own brand, the page you use will carry the client's name rather than ours. We're still the ones behind it, and this policy still applies.

What we collect

  • Identity details: your name, date of birth, residential address and contact details
  • Identity documents: for example a driver licence or passport, and the numbers and details they contain
  • Biometric information, where a verification method uses it: for example a photo or short video of your face, compared against the photo on your identity document. This is sensitive information, so we only collect it if you expressly agree at the time, after being told what will happen with it, and you can choose to be verified another way instead. The image is used for that comparison and the result is kept on the record; the image itself is deleted once the check is complete
  • Screening results: whether your name matches entries on sanctions lists, politically exposed person registers or media sources, and the material behind any match. This can include sensitive information - for example a political role or affiliation, or a criminal allegation or conviction reported in the media. We collect it without asking your consent because the anti-money laundering laws require or authorise the business you're dealing with to screen its customers, and we record only what is relevant to that screening. Anything a search turns up that has nothing to do with it, such as health information, isn't recorded
  • Information about the transaction or relationship that made the check necessary, to the extent our client provides it

Where it comes from

  • From you directly, when you provide documents or complete a verification step
  • From our client, who gives us your contact details and the context of the engagement
  • From the agency that issued your identity document. With your express consent, the document details are sent through the Australian Government's Document Verification Service to the issuing agency, which checks them against its records and returns only a match or no match - no other information about you comes back to us
  • From a credit reporting body, only if you expressly agree after we've explained the check. It confirms whether the name, address and date of birth you've given match its records - nothing more. It isn't a credit check or a credit application, and the credit reporting body isn't allowed to use or keep what we send it for any other purpose. Another way to verify is always available if you'd prefer it, and if the details don't match we'll tell you which credit reporting body was involved so you can contact it
  • From official registers and screening databases covering sanctions, politically exposed persons and adverse media

What we do with it

We use this information to verify your identity, carry out the screening and monitoring our client's obligations require, and prepare the verification record we hand back to them. We also use it, where we have to, for our own quality checks, to handle a complaint or legal claim, and to meet our own record-keeping obligations. We disclose it to:

  • The client who requested the check, who receives the verification record - the details checked, the screening results and the outcome - so it can meet its legal obligations
  • The verification and screening providers we use to perform the check, under contracts that require them to protect it
  • The providers that host our systems and store our records, under the same kind of contracts
  • Our professional advisers and insurers, if a complaint or claim makes that necessary
  • AUSTRAC, the financial crime regulator, through reports our client is required by law to make - reports we may help prepare - and other regulators, law enforcement or courts where the law requires or authorises disclosure

We do not use information collected for a verification for marketing, and we do not sell personal information. Government identifiers, such as passport or licence numbers, are used only to verify your identity, for the record of that verification, and for any report our client is required by law to make. We don't use them as our own reference number for you, and we only pass them to the verification service, the business that asked for the check, and anyone the law requires us to.

Automated checks

Some steps in a verification are carried out by computer programs. A program checks that the details on your identity document match the record held by the agency that issued it, compares your facial image with the photo on your document where you've agreed to that, and matches your name and date of birth against sanctions, politically exposed person and adverse media lists. These programs use your name, date of birth, address, document details and, where used, your facial image.

They don't make any decision on their own. A name match on a sanctions, politically exposed person or media list is not a finding about you - one of our analysts reviews every potential match before it's treated as real. Where software does part of a check, we don't record a failed verification on the software's result alone: a person looks at it, or we verify you another way. Whether to go ahead with you is our client's decision, not ours and not a program's. If any of that ever changes, we'll update this section first.

How long we keep it

The anti-money laundering laws require the business that asked for the check to keep a record of how you were identified for seven years, generally counted from the end of its relationship with you (or from the transaction, if it was a one-off). Because we hold that record so the business can meet its obligation, we can't delete it on request during that period, even if you'd like us to. What has to be kept is the record of the check: your name, date of birth and address, the type and number of the documents used, what we did to verify them, the screening results, the outcome, and anything else needed to show the check was done properly. The law doesn't require copies of your identity documents or your facial image to be kept once the check is complete, so once the record is finalised we securely delete them and only the details above stay on the record. When the retention period ends and the record is no longer required, it is securely destroyed or de-identified.

Access and correction

You're entitled to ask what personal information we hold about you and to ask for corrections, and we'll respond within 30 days. Because the record was created for a client's check, we may consult that client before responding, but the request is ours to answer and we won't simply send you elsewhere. A completed verification generally can't be altered, because the record must show what was actually checked at the time. If something was recorded inaccurately, we'll correct it where we can; if we can't, we'll attach a statement to the record setting out what you believe is wrong, and we'll tell the client about the change if you ask us to. If we refuse a request, we'll tell you why in writing and how to complain.

In limited situations the law prevents us from sharing certain information connected with a check, and can also limit what we're able to say about whether anything has been held back, or why. If that ever applies, you'll receive everything we're able to give you, and the rest will be handled as the law requires. Our contact details are at the end of this policy.

Part B - Clients, prospective clients and website visitors

What we collect

  • Contact and enquiry details you give us: your name, firm, sector, email, phone and anything you include in a message to us or a booking
  • Engagement records: the information needed to scope, deliver and administer our services, including billing details
  • Website data: our own pages don't run analytics or advertising trackers, and we don't collect anything about you ourselves while you browse. Our website is hosted by GitHub Pages, which logs the IP address of every visitor for security purposes - GitHub doesn't publish how long it keeps those logs, we have no access to them, and the hosting sets no cookies. Our contact page is the exception. As soon as it opens, two outside services receive your IP address and browser details: a Cloudflare bot check, and the Calendly booking calendar, which also loads Calendly's own analytics and marketing tools and sets cookies inside its frame. If you send the enquiry form, a small program we run on Cloudflare's network handles it and keeps a technical log of the request - your IP address, browser details and approximate location, not your message - for up to seven days. The next section explains each of these

We collect this information directly from you wherever practical. If we receive personal information we didn't ask for and don't need, we destroy or de-identify it.

You can browse our website and ask us general questions without telling us who you are. We can't verify an identity anonymously - that's the whole point of the check - and we'll need your details before we take on your firm as a client.

If you contact us through our website

Before you type anything

Two things happen as soon as our contact page opens. Cloudflare Turnstile runs a background check so we can tell a person from a bot when the form is sent. It receives your IP address and browser details, runs a few small tests in your browser, and gives your browser a one-time code that expires after five minutes. It doesn't read what you type into the form, and it sets no cookies on our site, though it may keep short-lived state inside its own frame while the check runs. Cloudflare uses what it collects to run the check for us and to improve its own bot detection, keeps it for a limited period it doesn't specify, and shows us summary figures - the countries, browsers and IP addresses it has seen. Cloudflare describes this in its Turnstile Privacy Addendum. The booking calendar also loads at this point - more on that below.

Sending a message

If you send us a message, the form travels over an encrypted connection to a small program we run on Cloudflare's network. The program passes the bot-check code and your IP address to Cloudflare to confirm the check was genuine, makes sure every field is filled in, and turns your message into an email to our Microsoft 365 mailbox. It doesn't keep a copy of anything you wrote. The email contains what you typed, plus the time it arrived, the page you sent it from, and the country your request appeared to come from - Cloudflare's estimate from your IP address, which can be wrong or blank. Your IP address itself isn't in the email, and Microsoft never receives it.

Cloudflare does keep a technical log of each request to that program in our Cloudflare account for up to seven days, then deletes it automatically. The log records the time, what was requested, whether it succeeded, and Cloudflare's details about where the request came from - your IP address, browser details and approximate location - but not what you wrote. Cloudflare also keeps its own logs of traffic across its network for a limited period it doesn't specify. Cloudflare, Inc. is a US company; the program usually runs at a Cloudflare location near you, and Cloudflare holds these records on its systems in the United States and Europe.

The email lands in our Microsoft 365 mailbox, which Microsoft stores in its Australian data centres. Microsoft scans it for spam and malware as part of normal delivery, may route it through its wider network on the way, and keeps its standard mail-flow and security logs about it for a limited period - dates, addresses and the subject line, which includes your name, but not the content. Microsoft's support staff can be given limited, logged access under Microsoft's terms. When we delete an enquiry, Microsoft keeps a recoverable copy for 14 days before removing it. How long we keep enquiries is set out under "How long we keep it" below.

Booking a call

The booking calendar is provided by Calendly and loads inside a frame from calendly.com as soon as you open our contact page, whether or not you book. When it loads, Calendly receives your IP address, browser and device details, your time zone, the fact that the calendar is on nimbi.com.au, and any marketing tags in the web address you arrived on. It gives your browser an anonymous identifier, loads its own analytics, session-recording and marketing tools inside the frame - including Google Analytics and a Meta pixel - and sets cookies and similar storage inside the frame, on calendly.com rather than on our site. Calendly uses that data for its own purposes, described in its Privacy Notice. We've switched off the cookie banner Calendly would otherwise show inside the embedded calendar, so there is no way to decline those cookies within it. If you'd rather make that choice, use the link under the calendar to open the booking page in a new tab, where Calendly shows its own cookie settings - or skip the contact page altogether and email us at info@nimbi.com.au.

If you book a call, Calendly collects your name and email address, the time you choose and your time zone, and any guest email addresses or note you add. It doesn't ask for a phone number. Calendly stores the booking on its systems in the United States, shares it with us, sends you the confirmation and reminder emails for the meeting, and lets any guests you add know. The booking form also asks you to accept Calendly's own terms. For your booking details Calendly acts on our behalf, so questions, corrections and deletion requests come to us: we can have Calendly delete a booking record, and it does so within seven days of our request.

How we use it

  • To respond to your enquiry and provide our services
  • To administer engagements: scheduling, invoicing, record keeping and quality review
  • To meet our own legal and professional obligations
  • To improve our services, using what we learn from engagements in a way that doesn't identify you
  • To send you information about our services or regulatory developments - but only if you've asked for it, or you're a client or have enquired with us and the message relates to that. Every message will say it's from Nimbi, tell you how to contact us, and include a working unsubscribe. We'll stop within five business days of an opt-out - usually the same day - we'll never charge you for opting out, and you can ask us where we got your details

Who we share it with

We don't sell personal information, and we don't share it except as set out here:

  • Service providers who support our business, such as hosting, email, scheduling and document management providers, under arrangements that protect your information
  • Professional advisers, such as our lawyers, accountants or insurers, where needed
  • Regulators, law enforcement or courts, where the law requires or authorises it
  • A purchaser or successor, if our business or part of it is ever sold or restructured, on terms that continue to protect the information

How long we keep it

If you enquire and we don't end up working together, we delete your enquiry - and any booking record held for us by Calendly - within twelve months. If you become a client, we keep engagement records for seven years after the engagement ends, because company law and our professional and insurance obligations require records to be kept for that long. When information is no longer needed for any of these purposes, we securely destroy or de-identify it.

Where your information goes

This section applies to everyone covered by this policy. The verification records we hold for clients' checks, and our own client files, are stored with providers that keep them in Australia.

The screening databases we use for sanctions, politically exposed person and media checks are run by specialist providers that operate globally, and the details we search - such as your name and date of birth - may be processed outside Australia. We'll tell you which countries are involved if you ask.

If you use our website, some of your information goes to the overseas companies that run it. Our pages are hosted by GitHub, a US company. They're usually delivered from a location in Australia, but GitHub says it stores and processes data in the United States and in other countries where it or its providers operate, and its content-delivery provider, Fastly, processes data in the United States. The bot check on our contact page and the program that handles the enquiry form run on Cloudflare's network; Cloudflare, Inc. is a US company that processes this data in the United States and Europe. The booking calendar is run by Calendly, a US company that stores bookings in the United States and uses service providers almost all of which are in the United States, with a few in the Netherlands, Israel and the United Kingdom. Our email is hosted by Microsoft 365. Microsoft keeps our mailboxes in its Australian data centres, but may pass email through its wider network for routing and security scanning, and its support staff can be given limited, logged access under Microsoft's terms.

Before personal information goes to a provider outside Australia, we take reasonable steps to make sure it will be handled in line with the Australian Privacy Principles, including terms in our contracts that require the provider to protect it. Whoever handles your information for us, in Australia or overseas, we remain responsible for how they handle it, and you can bring any concern about it to us.

Security

We protect personal information with access controls that limit it to the people who need it, encryption on the systems where we store verification records and client files, and providers chosen for their security. Everyone at Nimbi is trained in handling personal information. Messages and booking notes sent through our website are encrypted on the way but end up as an email in an ordinary inbox or as a booking on Calendly's systems, so please keep identity document details and other sensitive information out of them - if an engagement needs them, we'll give you a secure way to send them.

If a data breach happens that is likely to cause serious harm to the people affected, we'll notify them and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, as the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme requires, and we'll say what we're doing about it. Where the information was collected for a client's check, we'll also tell that client straight away - the law treats both of us as holding that information, and we'll agree with them who contacts you, so you're told once and clearly.

Access, correction and complaints

You can contact us at any time to ask what personal information we hold about you, request a copy, ask for corrections, withdraw a consent you've given, or opt out of marketing. We'll need to confirm your identity first, and we'll respond within 30 days. If we can't do what you've asked - for example because the law requires us to keep a record - we'll tell you why in writing.

If you have a concern about how we've handled your information, tell our Privacy Officer first. We'll acknowledge your complaint within seven days, look into it, and give you a written answer within 30 days - or tell you why we need longer. If you're not satisfied with our answer, you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC), which generally expects you to have raised the matter with us first. You can lodge a complaint online at oaic.gov.au, call 1300 363 992, or write to GPO Box 5288, Sydney NSW 2001.
