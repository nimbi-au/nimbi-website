Privacy Policy and Collection Notice

This policy explains how Nimbi Group Pty Ltd (ABN 36 701 758 018), trading as Nimbi AML ("Nimbi AML", "we", "us"), collects, uses, discloses and protects personal information. We provide an identity verification platform (Nimbi Lens) to businesses regulated under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006 (the AML/CTF Act), such as accounting, legal, conveyancing and real estate practices, and we carry out due diligence and screening as an agent for some of those businesses, for example when we map the risk of a client's existing customer book. We handle the personal information involved in that work in line with the Australian Privacy Principles, and we apply the same standards to everything else we do. This policy covers all of it.

Because of what we do, we handle personal information in two quite different situations, and this policy is split the same way. Part A covers people whose identity is verified, or whose name is screened, through Nimbi AML on behalf of a client. Part B covers our clients, prospective clients and website visitors.

The short version

If a business you're dealing with has asked you to verify your identity through Nimbi AML, or has asked us to screen your name, that's because the law requires it to check who it's dealing with. We collect what the check needs, share the outcome with that business, keep the record for as long as the law requires, and don't use your information for anything beyond that check and our own record-keeping. The check runs on our verification provider's platform, which stores the information in Germany.

If you're a client or visiting our website, we collect what you give us, use it to respond and to deliver our services, never sell it, and you can ask us at any time what we hold and to correct it.

Part A - People verified or screened through Nimbi AML on behalf of a client

Why we hold your information

A business you are dealing with - for example an accounting practice, law firm, conveyancer, real estate agency, buyers' agent or property developer - may use Nimbi AML's verification platform to check your identity, or engage Nimbi AML to carry out customer due diligence on its behalf. Either way, we hold your information on that business's behalf. The AML/CTF Act and its Rules require that business to identify and verify its customers before providing certain services, to work out who owns and controls any company, trust or other organisation it acts for, to identify anyone acting on a customer's behalf, and to keep monitoring the relationship for as long as it continues. So you may hear from us, or from the business through a page carrying our name, because you're the customer, or because you're connected to one - a director, a trustee, someone authorised to act for a company or trust, or someone who owns or controls it.

The business should point you to this notice before or when your details are collected. If the information can't be collected, or your identity can't be verified, the business generally can't go ahead with the service you've asked for. If you have questions about why a check is needed, the business that asked for it is the right first contact: the obligation is theirs, and the decision to provide or decline their service is theirs too.

Sometimes a client asks us to screen the customers it already has rather than someone new. Your details then come to us from the client, not from you, and you may never hear from us directly. The client's own privacy notice should tell you that checks like this happen; this policy explains what we do with your information once it reaches us.

Where a client runs the verification journey under its own brand, the page you use will carry the client's name rather than ours. The platform behind it is ours, and this policy still applies.

What we collect

  • Identity details: your name, date of birth, residential address and contact details
  • Identity documents: for example a driver licence or passport, the numbers and details they contain, and images of the document
  • Biometric information: a face image and a short video used to compare you with the photo on your identity document and to confirm you are a live person. Biometric information is sensitive information. We collect it only with your consent, which we ask for on the screen before the check starts. If you do not consent, the business you're dealing with can verify you another way. The video and any biometric template are deleted once the verification result is final; the result stays on the record
  • Device and connection details: IP address, device type and location signals captured when you complete a check, used to detect fraud
  • Business verification details, where a check covers a company or trust: the names and details of directors, officers, trustees and beneficial owners, drawn from public registers and corporate records as well as from our client
  • Screening results: whether your name matches entries on sanctions lists, politically exposed person registers or media sources, and the material behind any match. This can include sensitive information - for example a political role or affiliation, or a criminal allegation or conviction reported in the media. We collect it without asking your consent because the anti-money laundering laws require or authorise the business you're dealing with to screen its customers, and we record only what is relevant to that screening. Anything a search turns up that has nothing to do with it, such as health information, isn't recorded
  • Information about the transaction or relationship that made the check necessary, to the extent our client provides it

Where it comes from

  • From you directly, when you provide documents or complete a verification step
  • From our client, who gives us your contact details and the context of the engagement and, for a company or trust, details of the people who own and control it
  • From our identity verification provider and the sources it uses to confirm what you've provided: document authenticity checks, the Australian Government's Document Verification Service, official company and business registers, and screening databases covering sanctions, politically exposed persons and adverse media

What we do with it

We use this information to verify your identity, carry out the screening and monitoring our client's obligations require, and prepare the verification record our client receives. We also use it, where we have to, for our own quality checks, to handle a complaint or legal claim, and to meet our own record-keeping obligations. We disclose it to:

  • The client who requested the check, who receives the verification record - the details checked, the screening results and the outcome - so it can meet its legal obligations
  • Our identity verification provider, which operates the verification platform and stores the information for us, and its sub-processors including cloud hosting providers, under contracts that require them to protect it
  • Screening data providers, to run and refresh checks against sanctions, politically exposed person and adverse media sources
  • The providers that host our own systems and records, under the same kind of contracts
  • Our professional advisers and insurers, if a complaint or claim makes that necessary
  • AUSTRAC and other regulators, law enforcement or courts, where the law requires or authorises disclosure, including through reports our client is required by law to make

We do not use information collected for a verification for marketing, and we do not sell personal information. Government identifiers, such as passport or licence numbers, are used only to verify your identity, for the record of that verification, and for any report our client is required by law to make. We don't use them as our own reference number for you, and we only pass them to the verification provider, the business that asked for the check, and anyone the law requires us to.

Where it is stored and how we protect it

Our identity verification provider stores and processes verification information, including identity document images and face images, on servers in Germany. Its sub-processors, including cloud hosting providers, are also located in Germany. Before we send information overseas we take reasonable steps to make sure it is handled in line with the Australian Privacy Principles. Those steps include binding contract terms, access controls and encryption.

Information is encrypted in transit and at rest. Access is limited to the people who need it and is logged. Where our client has engaged us to run its checks, our staff access your information only to do that work. Where our client runs its own checks on the platform, its authorised staff see the records for its own customers and nobody else's.

Automated checks

We use automated systems to check identity documents, compare your face image with the photo on your document, confirm that you are a live person, and match names against screening lists. These systems produce a verification result and screening alerts. The business you're dealing with, or we where that business has engaged us, uses the result to decide whether your identity has been verified and whether a screening alert is a true match. A person reviews results that fail or are unclear, and a name match on a screening list is not treated as a finding about you until a person has looked at it. Whether to go ahead with you is the business's decision, not ours and not a program's.

The kinds of personal information these systems use: name, date of birth, document details, document images, face image and video, device and connection details, and screening data. The kinds of decisions they make or help make: whether your identity is verified, whether a business and its ownership are verified, and whether a screening alert is a true match.

How long we keep it

The AML/CTF Act requires the business that asked for the check to keep records connected with customer due diligence for seven years. We count that period from the date the business tells us its relationship with you has ended, or from the date a one-off transaction is complete. During that period the record holds your name, date of birth and address, the details taken from your documents, the document images, the screening results and the outcome. Face video and any biometric template are deleted once the verification result is final. When the business's relationship with you ends, we give the business a copy of the verification record. Because we hold these records so the business can meet its legal obligation, we can't delete them on request during the retention period, even if you'd like us to, and we refer deletion requests about a check to the business whose check it was. When the retention period ends, the records are securely destroyed or de-identified.

Access and correction

You're entitled to ask what personal information we hold about you and to ask for corrections, and we'll respond within 30 days. Because the record was created for a client's check, we may consult that client before responding, but the request is ours to answer and we won't simply send you elsewhere. A completed verification generally can't be altered, because the record must show what was actually checked at the time. If something was recorded inaccurately, we'll correct it where we can; if we can't, we'll attach a statement to the record setting out what you believe is wrong, and we'll tell the client about the change if you ask us to. If we refuse a request, we'll tell you why in writing and how to complain.

In limited situations the law prevents us from sharing certain information connected with a check, and can also limit what we're able to say about whether anything has been held back, or why. If that ever applies, you'll receive everything we're able to give you, and the rest will be handled as the law requires.

Send requests to the privacy contact in Part B. If you have a concern about how we've handled your information, the complaints process in Part B applies to you too.

Part B - Clients, prospective clients and website visitors

What we collect

  • Contact and enquiry details you give us: your name, firm, sector, email, phone and anything you include in a message to us or a booking
  • Engagement records: the information needed to scope, deliver and administer our services, including billing details
  • Website data: the readiness check on our site runs entirely in your browser; your answers are not sent to us, stored or attached to any enquiry. Our own pages don't run analytics or advertising trackers, and we don't collect anything about you ourselves while you browse. Our website is hosted by GitHub Pages, which logs visitor IP addresses for security and sets no cookies. Our contact page is the exception: a Cloudflare bot check and the Calendly booking calendar both load as soon as it opens, and both receive your IP address and browser details. The next section explains them

We collect this information directly from you wherever practical. If we receive personal information we didn't ask for and don't need, we destroy or de-identify it.

You can browse our website and ask us general questions without telling us who you are. We can't verify an identity anonymously - that's the whole point of the check - and we'll need your details before we take on your firm as a client.

If you contact us through our website

Sending a message

Our contact form is protected by Cloudflare Turnstile, a bot check that runs as soon as the page opens. It receives your IP address and browser details, sets no cookies on our site, and doesn't read what you type. Cloudflare describes what it does with that information in its Turnstile Privacy Addendum.

When you send a message it travels over an encrypted connection to a small program we run on Cloudflare's network, which confirms the bot check, validates the form and turns your message into an email to our mailbox. It keeps no copy of what you wrote, and your IP address isn't included in the email. Cloudflare keeps a technical log of the request - not its contents - for up to seven days, then deletes it. Cloudflare, Inc. is a US company. Our mailbox is hosted by Microsoft 365, which stores it in Australian data centres and scans mail for spam and malware as part of normal delivery.

Booking a call

The booking calendar is provided by Calendly and loads inside a frame from calendly.com as soon as you open our contact page, whether or not you book. Calendly receives your IP address, browser and device details and time zone, runs its own analytics and marketing tools inside the frame, and sets cookies there - on calendly.com, not on our site - for its own purposes, described in its Privacy Notice. We've switched off the cookie banner Calendly would otherwise show inside the embedded calendar, so there is no way to decline those cookies within it. If you'd rather make that choice, use the link under the calendar to open the booking page in a new tab, where Calendly shows its own cookie settings - or skip the contact page altogether and email us at info@nimbi.com.au.

If you book a call, Calendly collects your name and email address, the time you choose and your time zone, and any guest addresses or note you add. It stores the booking on its systems in the United States, shares it with us, and sends the confirmation and reminder emails. For your booking details Calendly acts on our behalf, so questions, corrections and deletion requests come to us: we can have Calendly delete a booking record, and it does so within seven days of our request.

How we use it

  • To respond to your enquiry and provide our services
  • To administer engagements: scheduling, invoicing, record keeping and quality review
  • To meet our own legal and professional obligations
  • To improve our services, using what we learn from engagements in a way that doesn't identify you
  • To send you information about our services or regulatory developments - but only if you've asked for it, or you're a client or have enquired with us and the message relates to that. Every message will say it's from Nimbi AML, tell you how to contact us, and include a working unsubscribe. We'll stop within five business days of an opt-out - usually the same day - we'll never charge you for opting out, and you can ask us where we got your details

Who we share it with

We don't sell personal information, and we don't share it except as set out here:

  • Service providers who support our business, such as hosting, email, scheduling and document management providers, under arrangements that protect your information
  • Professional advisers, such as our lawyers, accountants or insurers, where needed
  • Regulators, law enforcement or courts, where the law requires or authorises it
  • A purchaser or successor, if our business or part of it is ever sold or restructured, on terms that continue to protect the information

How long we keep it

If you enquire and we don't end up working together, we delete your enquiry - and any booking record held for us by Calendly - within twelve months. If you become a client, we keep engagement records for seven years after the engagement ends, because company law and our professional and insurance obligations require records to be kept for that long. When information is no longer needed for any of these purposes, we securely destroy or de-identify it.

Where your information goes

This section applies to everyone covered by this policy. Verification information for clients' checks, including identity document images and face images, is stored and processed by our identity verification provider on servers in Germany, as set out in Part A, and its sub-processors are also located in Germany. Our own client files and email are held in Australia.

The screening databases used for sanctions, politically exposed person and media checks are run by specialist providers that operate globally, and the details searched - such as your name and date of birth - may be processed outside Australia. We'll tell you which countries are involved if you ask.

If you use our website, some of your information goes to the overseas companies that run it. Our pages are hosted by GitHub, a US company that stores and processes data in the United States and other countries where it and its providers operate. The bot check and enquiry form on our contact page run on Cloudflare's network; Cloudflare, Inc. is a US company that processes this data in the United States and Europe. The booking calendar is run by Calendly, a US company that stores bookings in the United States. Our email is hosted by Microsoft 365, which keeps our mailboxes in Australian data centres but may route mail through its wider network for delivery and security scanning.

Before personal information goes to a provider outside Australia, we take reasonable steps to make sure it will be handled in line with the Australian Privacy Principles, including terms in our contracts that require the provider to protect it. Whoever handles your information for us, in Australia or overseas, we remain responsible for how they handle it, and you can bring any concern about it to us.

Security

We protect personal information with access controls that limit it to the people who need it, encryption in transit and at rest on the systems where verification records and client files are stored, and providers chosen for their security. Everyone at Nimbi AML is trained in handling personal information. Messages and booking notes sent through our website are encrypted on the way but end up as an email in an ordinary inbox or as a booking on Calendly's systems, so please keep identity document details and other sensitive information out of them - if an engagement needs them, we'll give you a secure way to send them.

If a data breach happens that is likely to cause serious harm to the people affected, we'll notify them and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, as the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme requires, and we'll say what we're doing about it. Where the information was collected for a client's check, we'll also tell that client straight away - the law treats both of us as holding that information, and we'll agree with them who contacts you, so you're told once and clearly.

Access, correction and complaints

You can contact us at any time to ask what personal information we hold about you, request a copy, ask for corrections, withdraw a consent you've given, or opt out of marketing. We'll need to confirm your identity first, and we'll respond within 30 days. If we can't do what you've asked - for example because the law requires us to keep a record - we'll tell you why in writing.

If you have a concern about how we've handled your information, tell our Privacy Officer first. We'll acknowledge your complaint within five business days, look into it, and give you a written answer within 30 days - or tell you why we need longer. If you're not satisfied with our answer, you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC), which generally expects you to have raised the matter with us first. You can lodge a complaint online at oaic.gov.au, call 1300 363 992, or write to GPO Box 5288, Sydney NSW 2001.
