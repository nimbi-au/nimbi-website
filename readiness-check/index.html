Step 1 of 2 · Your services

Part 1 · Your services

What work does your business do?

Choose your sector and answer for work carried out in the course of business. These questions identify matters to check against the Act and Rules; they are not a legal determination. A geographical link to Australia and any relevant exclusions or exemptions must also be considered.

Do you help a client plan or carry out a sale, purchase or transfer of real estate?

Consider work that directly advances a transaction, rather than general advice alone. Services pursuant to or resulting from a court or tribunal order have an exclusion; earlier work needs its own assessment.

Do you help a client buy, sell or transfer a controlling interest in a legal entity or arrangement?

Assess the transaction and the role you perform. A hypothetical sale or general tax advice alone is different from acting to progress a transaction.

Do you receive, hold, control or manage client money or property to assist a transaction?

Relevant exceptions include payment for your own services and specified court or government payments. Incidental handling for non-designated work is conditional and depends on the business providing no other designated services beyond this client-property service. Select Not sure if an exception may apply.

Do you help organise or carry out equity or debt financing for a legal entity or arrangement?

Include proposed entities. The work must directly advance the financing, rather than only provide general information about options.

Do you help create or restructure legal entities or arrangements, including SMSF trusts?

The scope includes relevant preparatory steps. Testamentary trusts are excluded. Whether detailed advice itself advances a structure depends on what it enables the client to do.

Do you act as, or arrange someone to act as, an officeholder, trustee, partner or nominee shareholder for a client?

Other specified positions, including attorneys of entities, may also be relevant. Court-ordered roles and an attorney for an individual require separate consideration under the precise service definition.

Do you provide a registered office or principal business address for a client entity, or sell or transfer a shelf company?

These are separate designated-service categories. Check the exact activities performed and any applicable exemptions.

Do you broker property sales or transfers for sellers, for commission or other consideration?

Both buyer and seller are customers of the brokerage service. The definition covers specified interests in Australian land, including leases longer than 30 years; it excludes some other interests.

Do you act as a buyers’ agent to find, negotiate or buy real estate for a fee or commission?

The buyer relationship starts when the agreement to find or identify a property is signed. The seller is also a customer when the transaction is reasonably expected to proceed.

Does your business sell or transfer real estate directly to buyers, without an independent agent brokering the sale, as a business of selling real estate?

Include off-the-plan property, subdivisions and sales through in-house staff. Construction alone and a one-off sale of your own premises are different. Assess the selling entity; select Not sure if a related entity or agency arrangement makes the position unclear.

Part 2 · Your arrangements

What is in place today?

Answer for your business. Select “Not sure” where you need to check the evidence or clarify the requirement.

01 Enrolment. Is your business enrolled and its AUSTRAC information current?
02 Risk assessment. Have you documented and reviewed the risks your business faces?
03 Written program. Do staff follow documented policies and procedures?
04 Approval and oversight. Is the program approved by a senior manager and overseen by your governing body?
05 Compliance officer. Is an appropriate management-level officer appointed and carrying out the role?
06 Initial CDD. Do you establish customer identity, ownership, purpose and risk before service, subject to permitted exceptions?
07 Ongoing CDD. Do you review changes, update information and address unusual activity?
08 Existing customer reviews. Have you recorded your existing customers’ risk ratings, where required, and set risk-based periodic CDD review dates?

Use your program’s categories, such as low, medium or high. Apply the special rules for pre-commencement customers and review sooner when circumstances change.

09 Reporting. Can your team identify, assess and submit required reports within applicable deadlines?
10 Records and people. Are records retained and relevant personnel checked and trained?
11 Independent evaluation. Have you arranged evaluation within the applicable timetable?
