Quick readiness check

Two parts. First, whether the AML/CTF Act may apply to your practice. Second, how ready you are. Your answers stay in this page in your browser; nothing is sent, stored or attached to an enquiry.

Part 1. Does the Act apply?

Choose your sector. Each question is a designated service in the Act, with its main conditions. Answer for work you do in the course of business, whether or not you charge for it.


Do you help clients plan or carry out buying, selling or transferring real estate, other than under a court or tribunal order?
Why this mattersTable 6 item 1. Advice that only influences a decision is not a designated service; acting in the transaction is.
Do you help clients plan or carry out the sale, purchase or transfer of a controlling interest in a company, trust or other legal arrangement, other than under a court or tribunal order?
Why this mattersTable 6 item 2. Tax advice on a possible sale is not captured until you are instructed to act in a transaction with a buyer in view.
Does client money or property pass through your accounts, including a trust account, or do you deal with it on the client's instructions, as part of a transaction?
Why this mattersTable 6 item 3. Excludes money held only as payment for your services, incidental holdings for non-designated work, and receipts or payments under a court order.
Do you help organise or carry out equity or debt financing for a company or legal arrangement?
Why this mattersTable 6 item 4.
Do you help create or restructure companies, trusts, SMSFs or other legal arrangements, including drafting the deed or constitution or registering with ASIC?
Why this mattersTable 6 item 6. Testamentary trusts are excluded.
Do you act as, or arrange for someone to act as, a director, secretary, trustee, partner or nominee shareholder for a client?
Why this mattersTable 6 items 7 and 8. Court-ordered fiduciary roles and attorneys for natural persons are excluded.
Do you provide a registered office or principal place of business address for a client entity, or sell shelf companies?
Why this mattersTable 6 items 5 and 9.
Do you act for clients in transactions to buy, sell or transfer real estate, including conveyancing, other than under a court or tribunal order?
Why this mattersTable 6 item 1. Litigation and advice on completed transactions are not captured.
Do you act for clients in the sale, purchase or transfer of a controlling interest in a company, trust or other legal arrangement, other than under a court or tribunal order?
Why this mattersTable 6 item 2.
Do you receive, hold, control or pay out client money or property as part of a transaction, including through your trust account?
Why this mattersTable 6 item 3. Excludes money held only as payment for your services, incidental holdings where you provide no other designated service, and receipts or payments under a court order.
Do you help organise or carry out equity or debt financing for a company or legal arrangement?
Why this mattersTable 6 item 4.
Do you help create or restructure companies, trusts or other legal arrangements?
Why this mattersTable 6 item 6. Wills and testamentary trusts are excluded.
Do you act as, or arrange, a director, secretary, trustee, partner, attorney of an entity or nominee shareholder for a client?
Why this mattersTable 6 items 7 and 8.
Do you provide a registered office or principal place of business address for a client entity, or sell shelf companies?
Why this mattersTable 6 items 5 and 9.
Do you act for sellers to sell or transfer real estate, for a commission or other consideration?
Why this mattersTable 5 item 1, seller's agent. Real estate includes freehold and leases of more than 30 years; leases of 30 years or less are excluded.
Do you act for buyers to find, negotiate or buy real estate, for a fee or commission?
Why this mattersTable 5 item 1, buyer's agent. The service starts when the agreement to find a property is signed.
Does your business sell or transfer real estate it develops or owns directly to buyers, without an independent real estate agent brokering the sale, as a business of selling real estate: for example off-the-plan apartments, house-and-land packages or subdivided lots, including through in-house sales staff?
Why this mattersTable 5 item 2. Answer for the entity that actually sells. If an independent agent brokers the sale, that agent provides the brokerage service (item 1) and item 2 does not apply to your business for that sale. Construction alone, or a one-off sale of your own premises, is not a business of selling real estate. If a related entity does the selling or the arrangement is unclear, answer Not sure.

Tax returns, financial statements, BAS and bookkeeping are not designated services on their own.

Legal advice, litigation and dispute resolution on their own are generally not designated services.

Part 2. How ready are you?

One question per theme. Yes, no, or not sure. The result separates what you have said needs attention from what needs clarifying.

01 Enrol with AUSTRAC. Is the practice enrolled, with the compliance officer notified?
02 Assess your risk. Is there a written, current assessment of the practice's ML/TF risks, with the reasoning recorded?
03 Keep a written program. Are there written AML/CTF policies and procedures that staff actually follow?
04 Approve and oversee it. Has a senior manager approved the program, and does a governing body oversee it?
05 Compliance officer. Is a fit and proper person at management level appointed and doing the role?
06 Know your customer before you start. Before a designated service begins, do you establish who the customer is, who owns or controls an entity, and check for PEPs and sanctions, with the evidence recorded?
07 Keep watching. Do you update customer information, re-check screening and act on activity that does not fit what you know?
08 Report when required. Could the practice recognise, assess and lodge a suspicious matter report inside the statutory time, and lodge the annual compliance report?
09 Records, personnel and training. Are due diligence and program records kept for the required periods, and are people in AML/CTF roles checked and trained as the program sets out?
10 Independent evaluation. Is an independent evaluation scheduled within the transitional deadline and at least every three years after?
