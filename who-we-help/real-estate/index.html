AML/CTF for real estate professionals

Real estate agents, buyers' agents and developers selling their own developments, including off the plan, provide designated services. For developers, the scope check considers whether an independent agent brokers the sale. Rent-only property management is not a designated service.

When the rules may apply

The real estate services in table 5 of the Act are:

  • brokering the sale, purchase or transfer of real estate on behalf of a buyer, seller, transferee or transferor in the course of business. AUSTRAC treats a broker as someone who acts as an intermediary or agent for consideration, typically negotiating or finding a counterparty for a commission. This covers seller's agents and buyer's agents, and both the buyer and the seller are your customers for the same transaction
  • selling or transferring real estate in the course of a business of selling real estate, where no independent real estate agent brokers the sale: for example a developer selling house and land packages, off-the-plan apartments or subdivided lots, including through its own in-house agents or sales staff. The buyer is the customer. Where an independent agent brokers a developer's sale, that agent provides the brokerage service and this item does not apply to the developer for that sale. Developing or building on its own, and a one-off sale of your own premises, are not a business of selling real estate.

Real estate means freehold, leasehold interests of more than 30 years and land use entitlements; leases of 30 years or less, easements, mortgagee interests and dwellings on leased sites are excluded. Transfers for no consideration still count, and the Act applies where the service has a geographical link to Australia. A seller's agent starts providing the service to the seller when the agency agreement is signed, and to the buyer when the sale is reasonably expected to proceed, typically on exchange. A buyer's agent starts with the buyer when the agreement to find a property is signed. A developer selling direct starts when there is a commitment to sell, typically on entering the sale agreement. AUSTRAC accepts delayed initial due diligence in limited circumstances, such as after an auction where completing it first would disrupt the ordinary course of business and the extra risk is low; the delay provisions are conditional, so a developer selling off the plan should not assume it can wait until settlement.

Examples

An agency lists and sells a house on a commission: a designated service, with the seller a customer from listing and the buyer a customer from exchange.

A buyers' agent signs an agreement to find a property for a client: a designated service from that signing, before any property is identified.

The same agency manages a rent roll: collecting rent, handling tenancies and maintenance are not designated services.

A developer sells apartments off the plan from its display suite through its own sales staff: a designated service, with each buyer a customer. If the same developer appoints an independent agent to broker the sales, the agent provides the brokerage service and the developer is not providing item 2 for those sales.

An owner sells their own business premises directly to a buyer, as a one-off: not a business of selling real estate, so not a designated service. A builder that constructs homes for a landowner and sells nothing is not providing a real estate designated service.

Your obligations in practice

The obligations are the same ten themes for every practice; only a few notes differ by sector. They are set out once, with the notes for real estate businesses.

Know

Assess the risk your services, clients, channels and countries carry.

Set

Write the program: who does what, by when, approved by a senior manager.

Run

Check who you deal with, keep watching, report when the law requires, keep records.

Prove

Show AUSTRAC and an independent evaluator that the rules were followed.

Ten themes, grouped for readability. They are an editorial grouping of the Act and the AML/CTF Rules 2025, not a count of every statutory duty, and how each applies depends on the services you provide.

  1. Enrol with AUSTRAC. Apply to enrol no later than 28 days after you start providing a designated service, and update your details within 14 days of a change. Notify AUSTRAC of your compliance officer within the required time.
  2. Assess your risk. Identify and assess the money laundering, terrorism financing and proliferation financing risks of your services, customers, delivery channels and the countries involved, and write down why. Review it when things change and periodically.
  3. Keep a written program. AML/CTF policies, procedures, systems and controls that fit the nature, size and complexity of your practice, in place before you provide a designated service and followed in daily work.
  4. Approve and oversee it. A senior manager approves the program and its updates; a governing body oversees it. In a small practice one person may hold more than one role.
  5. Appoint a compliance officer. A fit and proper person at management level who oversees day-to-day compliance and reports to the governing body.
  6. Know your customer before you start. Before the designated service begins, establish on reasonable grounds who the customer is, who owns or controls a company or trust, who acts for them, whether anyone is a politically exposed person or subject to sanctions, and the nature and purpose of the relationship. Collect and verify what the customer's risk calls for. Simplified measures are available only where the risk is low, enhanced measures are not required and your policies provide for them; enhanced measures, including source of funds and wealth where relevant, are required where the Act triggers them, including high risk, a foreign politically exposed person or a suspicion you continue to act through.
  7. Keep watching. Ongoing due diligence for as long as the relationship lasts: monitor for unusual transactions and behaviour, update information, re-check screening and respond to changes in risk. Clients you already had before 1 July 2026 are covered by the Act's provisions for pre-commencement customers rather than full initial due diligence from day one; check those provisions before re-verifying your whole client book.
  8. Report when required. A suspicious matter report where you suspect on reasonable grounds that a customer is not who they claim to be or that the service relates to money laundering, terrorism financing, proceeds of crime or another offence: within three business days after the day you form the suspicion, or 24 hours if it concerns terrorism financing. An annual compliance report. A threshold transaction report applies if your business receives or pays physical currency of $10,000 or more in a designated service, for example a deposit paid in notes; ordinary electronic payments are not threshold transactions. An unusual event or a screening match is not automatically a report; a person assesses whether there are reasonable grounds.
  9. Keep records, check and train people. Due diligence records for seven years after the relationship ends or the last occasional transaction, and program and other required records for the periods the Act sets. Risk-based due diligence on personnel in AML/CTF roles, and training appropriate to those roles, at a frequency your program sets.
  10. Independent evaluation. At least once every three years, at a frequency your policies set; transitional rules stagger the first one for newly regulated practices. Act on the findings.
