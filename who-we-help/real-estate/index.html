Start with the work you do

The services you provide decide your obligations under the AML/CTF Act and Rules. Start with the work below, then check the exact definitions, the link to Australia and any exemptions.

Sales agents

You broker a sale, purchase or transfer. Both the buyer and the seller are your customers, even if you act for one.

Buyers’ agents

You act for a buyer to find or buy property. The relationship usually starts when the agreement is signed.

Developers selling directly

You sell your own developments as a business, including off the plan, with no independent agent brokering the sale.

Outside these services

Rent-only property management is outside these designated services. Developing or building alone is not enough; how the property is sold matters.

Who is the customer, and when do checks start?
  • brokering the sale, purchase or transfer of real estate on behalf of a buyer, seller, transferee or transferor in the course of business. AUSTRAC treats a broker as someone who acts as an intermediary or agent for consideration, typically negotiating or finding a counterparty for a commission. This covers seller's agents and buyer's agents, and both the buyer and the seller are your customers for the same transaction
  • selling or transferring real estate in the course of a business of selling real estate, where no independent real estate agent brokers the sale: for example a developer selling house and land packages, off-the-plan apartments or subdivided lots, including through its own in-house agents or sales staff. The buyer is the customer. Where an independent agent brokers a developer's sale, that agent provides the brokerage service and this item does not apply to the developer for that sale. Developing or building on its own, and a one-off sale of your own premises, are not a business of selling real estate.

Real estate means freehold, leasehold interests of more than 30 years and land use entitlements; leases of 30 years or less, easements, mortgagee interests and dwellings not attached to land where the occupier owns the dwelling but leases the site are excluded; a leasehold interest over 30 years may itself qualify. Transfers for no consideration still count, and the Act applies where the service has a geographical link to Australia. A seller's agent starts providing the service to the seller when the agency agreement is signed, and to the buyer when the sale is reasonably expected to proceed, typically on exchange. A buyer's agent starts with the buyer when the agreement to find a property is signed. A developer selling direct starts when there is a commitment to sell, typically on entering the sale agreement. AUSTRAC accepts delayed initial due diligence in limited circumstances, such as after an auction where completing it first would disrupt the ordinary course of business and the extra risk is low; the delay provisions are conditional, so a developer selling off the plan should not assume it can wait until settlement.

Examples:

An agency lists and sells a house on a commission: a designated service, with the seller a customer from listing and the buyer a customer from exchange.

A buyers' agent signs an agreement to find a property for a client: a designated service from that signing, before any property is identified.

The same agency manages a rent roll: collecting rent, handling tenancies and maintenance are not designated services.

A developer sells apartments off the plan from its display suite through its own sales staff: a designated service, with each buyer a customer. If the same developer appoints an independent agent to broker the sales, the agent provides the brokerage service and the developer is not providing item 2 for those sales.

An owner sells their own business premises directly to a buyer, as a one-off: not a business of selling real estate, so not a designated service. A builder that constructs homes for a landowner and sells nothing is not providing a real estate designated service.

Your obligations in practice

Five practical stages group the key duties. Your services and circumstances determine the detail; this is a guide to the main themes, not an exhaustive legal checklist.

The obligationWhat your team does
  1. 01

    Confirm scope and enrol

    →

    Identify which services are regulated and put your firm on AUSTRAC’s roll.

    • Map the work you actually provide.
    • Apply to enrol and keep your details current.
    Scope and enrolment detail

    Enrol with AUSTRAC. If your business was already providing newly regulated designated services on 1 July 2026, the enrolment application deadline was 29 July 2026. If you genuinely start later, apply no later than 28 days after the day you first provide a designated service. This is not a grace period for your program or customer due diligence. Update enrolment details within 14 days of a change and notify AUSTRAC of your compliance officer within the required time. If overdue, apply now and prioritise the gaps in your compliance arrangements.

  2. 02

    Build and own the framework

    →

    Turn your firm’s risks into a program your people can use.

    • Assess the risks of your services and customer base.
    • Approve the program and assign oversight and a compliance officer.
    Risk, program and governance detail

    Assess your risk. Identify and assess the money laundering, terrorism financing and proliferation financing risks of your services, customers, delivery channels and the countries involved, and write down why. Review it when things change and periodically.

    Keep a written program. AML/CTF policies, procedures, systems and controls that fit the nature, size and complexity of your practice, in place before you provide a designated service and followed in daily work.

    Approve and oversee it. A senior manager approves the program and its updates; a governing body oversees it. In a small practice one person may hold more than one role.

    Appoint a compliance officer. A fit and proper person at management level who oversees day-to-day compliance and reports to the governing body.

  3. 03

    Know your customer

    →

    Establish who you are dealing with and understand the risk profile.

    • Check identity, ownership, authority and relationship purpose.
    • Apply the level of due diligence the risk and rules require.
    Customer due diligence detail

    Know your customer before you start. Before the designated service begins, subject to permitted exceptions, establish on reasonable grounds who the customer is, who owns or controls a legal entity or trust, who acts for them, whether anyone is a politically exposed person or subject to sanctions, and the nature and purpose of the relationship. Collect and verify what the customer's risk calls for. Simplified measures are available only where the risk is low, enhanced measures are not required and your policies provide for them; enhanced measures, including source of funds and wealth where relevant, are required where the Act triggers them, including high risk, a foreign politically exposed person or a suspicion you continue to act through.

    The fine print on simplified and enhanced CDD

    Simplified CDD is available only where the customer's ML/TF risk is low, enhanced CDD is not required and your AML/CTF policies set out the simplified measures; it is not an exemption from initial or ongoing CDD. Enhanced CDD is required when the Act triggers it, including high ML/TF risk, a foreign politically exposed person, a suspicious matter reporting obligation where you continue the service, and a FATF call-for-action jurisdiction, and it can apply in initial and ongoing CDD.

  4. 04

    Monitor and respond

    →

    Review changes, assess concerns and report when required.

    • Monitor activity and information relevant to the service and relationship.
    • Investigate alerts, document decisions and escalate concerns.
    Monitoring and reporting detail

    Keep watching. Ongoing due diligence for as long as the relationship lasts: monitor for unusual transactions and behaviour, update information, re-check screening and respond to changes in risk. Some existing customers qualify as pre-commencement customers, depending on the designated services and business relationship. Ongoing CDD still applies, and specified triggers require initial CDD. Check the transition requirements before re-verifying your client book.

    Report when required. A suspicious matter report where you suspect on reasonable grounds that a customer is not who they claim to be or that the service relates to money laundering, terrorism financing, proceeds of crime or another offence: within three business days after the day you form the suspicion, or 24 hours if it concerns terrorism financing. An annual compliance report for 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027 is due by 30 September 2027. Threshold transaction reports for reportable physical-currency transactions are due within 10 business days. Sector notes appear below. An unusual event or a screening match is not automatically a report; a person assesses whether there are reasonable grounds. Once a report is made or required, tipping-off rules restrict what you may tell the customer or anyone else; build this into your procedures. Legal professional privilege can affect reporting and timeframes for a legal practice, and for a conveyancer holding a lawyer's privileged advice.

    What about the clients we already have?

    Some existing customers qualify as pre-commencement customers, depending on the designated services and business relationship. Ongoing CDD still applies, and specified triggers require initial CDD. See AUSTRAC’s transition guidance. Nimbi Comprehensive maps and risk-rates your existing client book so you can see where the exposure sits before you act on it.

  5. 05

    Keep evidence and improve

    →

    Show what your team did and keep the framework effective.

    • Keep required records and train the people responsible.
    • Arrange independent evaluation and act on its findings.
    Records, people and evaluation detail

    Keep records, check and train people. Due diligence records for seven years after the relationship ends or the last occasional transaction, and program and other required records for the periods the Act sets. Risk-based due diligence on personnel in AML/CTF roles, and training appropriate to those roles, at a frequency your program sets.

    Independent evaluation. At least once every three years, at a frequency your policies set; transitional rules stagger the first one for newly regulated practices. Act on the findings. See the first-evaluation timetable for your AUSTRAC account number.

Reporting notes for your sector

A threshold transaction report applies if your business receives or pays physical currency of $10,000 or more in a designated service, for example a deposit paid in notes; ordinary electronic payments are not threshold transactions.

Recognise the risk in everyday work

Warning signs to look into, not findings of wrongdoing. Your team checks the facts and follows your procedures.

Warning sign

The person behind the buyer is unclear

A company buyer will not say who owns it or who is directing the purchase.

Your team’s response
Identify the owners and controllers, verify the authority to act, and record the risk rating.

Warning sign

Funding does not match the customer story

The money, or a third party paying, does not fit what you know about the customer.

Your team’s response
Ask for an explanation and evidence. Rate the risk again and escalate under your procedures.

Warning sign

The arrangement changes without explanation

The buyer or the source of funds changes late, with pressure to accept quickly.

Your team’s response
Review the new information and repeat any affected checks before you decide.
